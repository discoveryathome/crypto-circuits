# Second-wave handoff

This is an adaptive-method handoff, not an amendment to the frozen acceptance
rules, seeds, budgets or gate model. Read your own issued `start.json` and
`starting-frontier.json` for the authoritative clock and current starting points.
All correct first-wave submissions are known prior even if novelty review is
unresolved. Rediscovery earns no credit.

The first wave found useful Whirlwind candidates through local semantic
rewrites, associative rotations, randomized DAG orderings and bounded-window
resynthesis. Exact affine signal identities allowed sharing and cancellation.
This is a description of methods, not a novelty finding. The frozen original
toolkit's whole-map BP attempt consumed much of short runs on the denser
instances. A focused local method may make better use of this budget.

AES searches tried rotations, exact-distance windows, three-signal
resubstitution and a depth-three representation over small input supports.
No improvement was submitted at the time of this brief. Your issued frontier,
not this interim narrative, is authoritative.

Use the common original local toolkit, or implement your own session-local
methods. You may inspect inactive first-wave source and receipts as research
material, but do not execute another session's submitted code. Do not read or
write another active second-wave session. All new code, outputs and receipts
belong under your own session directory. Do not change trusted files.

Include every issued frontier circuit as an available start. Continuing from
your own verified improvements is permitted and expected; record added start
hashes. Derive the numeric seed programmatically from `start.json`; do not
transcribe a decimal seed manually. Record further deterministic seed
derivations. One first-wave attempt used a mistyped seed; it remains recorded
as a protocol deviation rather than being silently discarded.

Supervise every long compile/search command through `tools/budget_run.py`.
Use at most one search process at once. Time spent reasoning, coding and
self-checking is part of your 1,200-second elapsed cap. Reserve enough time to
save sources and receipts. Stop search at the issued deadline; distinguish
the last search-process end from the later summary-write time.
Prefer an inner search cutoff early enough for its wrapper to reap the child
and save CPU usage before the global supervisor fires. A first-wave forced
process-group shutdown lost final child CPU accounting; do not silently report
an incomplete receipt sum as total compute cost.

Submit strict circuit JSON atomically and check it with both trusted CLIs.
Preserve first checkpoint time, atomic submission time, seed, parent circuit
hash, method version and process log. Correctness and novelty are separate.
Keep failed experiments and known/duplicate findings in the session summary.
Do not idle to fill the budget or label an unsuccessful search an optimality
proof. Model and token costs remain unavailable unless directly exposed.
